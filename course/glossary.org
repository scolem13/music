* Music vocabulary

** a cappella
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: a-cappella
:SHORT: Vocal music performed without instrumental accompaniment.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: myp1 dp
:CATS: italian
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_cappella
:END:
Italian: "in the manner of the chapel." Choral or ensemble singing
performed entirely without instrumental accompaniment. The term is also
applied to solo vocal music without accompaniment. Common in Renaissance
sacred music, barbershop, and contemporary a cappella groups.

** accelerando
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: accelerando
:SHORT: Gradually getting faster.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: italian
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accelerando
:END:
Italian: "accelerating." A tempo direction indicating that the music
should gradually increase in speed. Abbreviated accel. The opposite
is ritardando (or rallentando): gradually slowing down.

*See also:* [[*tempo][tempo]], [[*rubato][rubato]]

** acciaccatura
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: acciaccatura
:SHORT: A very short ornamental note played just before the main note.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: italian
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acciaccatura
:END:
Italian: "crushing." A grace note written as a small note with a stroke
through its stem, played as briefly as possible immediately before the
main note — so quickly that it has almost no duration of its own.
Distinguished from the appoggiatura, which is held for a portion of the
main note's value and falls on the beat.

*See also:* [[*appoggiatura][appoggiatura]], [[*ornamentation][ornamentation]]

** acoustic instrument
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: acoustic-instrument
:SHORT: An instrument that produces sound by acoustic (non-electronic) means.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: general
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acoustic_music
:END:
An instrument that generates sound entirely through acoustic physical
processes — vibrating strings, air columns, membranes, or the body of
the instrument itself — without electronic amplification or signal
processing. Contrasted with electric instruments (which require
amplification) and electronic instruments (which generate sound
electronically).

*See also:* [[*aerophone][aerophone]], [[*chordophone][chordophone]], [[*idiophone][idiophone]]

** adagio
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: adagio
:SHORT: Slow and stately; a slow tempo marking.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: italian
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempo
:END:
Italian: "at ease." A tempo marking indicating a slow, stately pace —
slower than andante but faster than largo. Also used as a noun to
describe a slow movement (e.g., "the adagio of the symphony").

*See also:* [[*tempo][tempo]], [[*allegro][allegro]]

** ADSR
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: adsr
:SHORT: The four stages of a synthesizer envelope: Attack, Decay, Sustain, Release.
:IMPORTANCE: expert
:LEVELS: myp3 myp4 dp
:CATS: technology
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Envelope_(music)
:END:
The four parameters describing the shape of a synthesizer envelope —
the control signal that shapes how a sound changes over time. Attack
is the time to reach peak level; decay is the time to fall to the
sustain level; sustain is the held level while a key is pressed; release
is the time to fall to silence after the key is released. ADSR envelopes
typically control amplitude, filter cutoff, and pitch.

*See also:* [[*envelope curve][envelope curve]], [[*synthesis][synthesis]]

** aerophone
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: aerophone
:SHORT: An instrument that produces sound through a vibrating column of air.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: myp2 dp
:CATS: general
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerophone
:END:
One of the four primary instrument categories in the Hornbostel-Sachs
classification. Aerophones produce sound through the vibration of an
air column. Includes free aerophones (harmonica, accordion), where the
air vibrates without a tube, and tube aerophones (flute, trumpet,
oboe), where the air column is enclosed.

*See also:* [[*chordophone][chordophone]], [[*idiophone][idiophone]], [[*membranophone][membranophone]]

** alap
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: alap
:SHORT: The slow, unmetered introductory section of a raga performance.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: non-western
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alap
:END:
The opening section of a North Indian classical music performance, in
which the performer introduces and explores the notes and character of
the raga in free rhythm, without percussion accompaniment. The alap
establishes the raga's mood (rasa) before the metered sections begin.

*See also:* [[*raga][raga]], [[*bandish][bandish]]

** aleatoric/aleatory music
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: aleatoric-aleatory-music
:SHORT: Music incorporating elements of chance or unpredictability in its composition or performance.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: general
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleatoric_music
:END:
Music in which elements of chance or indeterminacy play a role — either
in the compositional process or in performance. Key figures include
John Cage (*Music of Changes*, 1951, composed using the I Ching) and
Witold Lutosławski (controlled aleatorism, where individual players
improvise within defined limits while the ensemble is coordinated).
Distinct from pure improvisation by the presence of some structural
framework.

*See also:* [[*improvising][improvising]], [[*chance music][chance music]]

** allegretto
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: allegretto
:SHORT: Moderately fast; slightly slower than allegro.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: italian
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempo
:END:
Italian: diminutive of allegro. A tempo marking indicating a moderately
brisk pace — lighter and slightly slower than allegro, but faster than
andante. Often implies a lighter character than allegro.

*See also:* [[*allegro][allegro]], [[*tempo][tempo]]

** allegro
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: allegro
:SHORT: Fast and lively; one of the most common tempo markings.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: italian
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempo
:END:
Italian: "merry, lively." A tempo marking indicating a fast, lively
pace. One of the most common movement designations in Western classical
music. Qualified forms include allegro ma non troppo (fast but not too
much), allegro vivace (fast and vivacious), and allegro moderato.

*See also:* [[*adagio][adagio]], [[*tempo][tempo]]

** antiphonal
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: antiphonal
:SHORT: Involving alternation between two groups of performers.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: myp4 dp
:CATS: general
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiphony
:END:
A performance practice in which two groups of performers alternate,
answer, or respond to each other. Common in Gregorian chant (antiphonal
chanting), Renaissance polychoral music (e.g., the double-choir works
of Giovanni Gabrieli at St. Mark's Venice), and gospel call-and-response
performance.

*See also:* [[*call and response][call and response]]

** appoggiatura
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: appoggiatura
:SHORT: An ornamental note on the beat that displaces the main note and resolves by step.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: italian
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appoggiatura
:END:
Italian: "leaning." A grace note that falls on the beat, displacing the
main note and taking a portion of its value before resolving by step
(usually downward). The appoggiatura creates a temporary dissonance that
adds expressive intensity. Distinguished from the acciaccatura by its
rhythmic weight — it is heard clearly rather than crushed through.

*See also:* [[*acciaccatura][acciaccatura]], [[*suspensions][suspensions]], [[*ornamentation][ornamentation]]

** arco
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: arco
:SHORT: A direction to resume playing with the bow (after pizzicato).
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: myp2 dp
:CATS: italian
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arco
:END:
Italian: "bow." A performance direction for string players indicating
that the bow should be used, typically appearing after a passage of
pizzicato (plucked) playing.

*See also:* [[*pizzicato][pizzicato]], [[*con arco][con arco]]

** aria
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: aria
:SHORT: A self-contained song for solo voice in an opera, oratorio, or cantata.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: italian
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aria
:END:
Italian: "air." A formal, self-contained composition for solo voice,
typically with orchestral accompaniment, in an opera, oratorio, or
cantata. Arias are usually more melodically elaborate than recitative
and allow the soloist to express a character's emotional state at length.
Common forms include the Baroque da capo aria (A–B–A structure).

*See also:* [[*recitative][recitative]], [[*cantata][cantata]], [[*opera][opera]]

** arpeggio
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: arpeggio
:SHORT: The notes of a chord played in succession rather than simultaneously.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: myp2 myp4 dp
:CATS: italian
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arpeggio
:END:
Italian: "in the manner of a harp." The notes of a chord played one
after another rather than all at once, either ascending or descending.
Arpeggios are used in both melody and accompaniment and are foundational
to techniques such as Alberti bass. Distinct from broken chord only in
the consistent direction of movement.

*See also:* [[*broken chord][broken chord]], [[*chord][chord]]

** atonal
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: atonal
:SHORT: Music that avoids establishing a tonal center.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: general
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atonality
:END:
Music that avoids organizing pitch around a central tonic, rejecting
the hierarchy of tonal harmony. Developed most systematically by
Schoenberg and the Second Viennese School in the early 20th century.
Free atonality (1908–1923) preceded the more systematic twelve-tone
technique. Atonal music uses all twelve chromatic pitches without
implying a home key.

*See also:* [[*12-note techniques][12-note techniques]], [[*tonality][tonality]], [[*non-tonal][non-tonal]]

** augmentation
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: augmentation
:SHORT: Restating a theme with all note values lengthened.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: harmony
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augmentation_(music)
:END:
A contrapuntal and developmental technique in which a melody or motif
is restated with all rhythmic values increased — typically doubled.
The opposite is diminution (values shortened). Augmentation is common
in fugue and in development sections.

*See also:* [[*diminution][diminution]], [[*fugue][fugue]], [[*development][development]]

** augmented
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: augmented
:SHORT: An interval or chord enlarged by a half step beyond its major or perfect form.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: general
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augmented_interval
:END:
An interval quality one semitone larger than a major or perfect interval
of the same number. An augmented fourth (tritone) is a half step larger
than a perfect fourth; an augmented sixth chord is a chromatic harmony
built on the flattened sixth degree. Augmented triads contain a major
third and an augmented fifth.

*See also:* [[*interval][interval]], [[*diminished][diminished]]

** authentic (perfect) cadence
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: authentic-perfect-cadence
:SHORT: A cadence moving from V to I — the strongest harmonic close in tonal music.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: general
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadence_(music)
:END:
The strongest cadential formula in tonal music: the dominant chord (V
or V7) resolving to the tonic (I). A perfect authentic cadence (PAC)
requires both chords in root position and the tonic note in the highest
voice at the point of resolution; an imperfect authentic cadence (IAC)
does not meet all these conditions. The V–I resolution is the
fundamental engine of tonal music.

*See also:* [[*cadence][cadence]], [[*plagal cadence][plagal cadence]], [[*V][V]]

** balophone
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: balophone
:SHORT: A West African xylophone-like instrument with gourd resonators beneath wooden bars.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: general
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balafon
:END:
A West African idiophone consisting of wooden bars of varying lengths
struck with mallets, with hollow gourd resonators suspended beneath each
bar. The balafon (also spelled balophone) is traditional in Mande, Gur,
and other West African musical cultures. A cousin of the marimba and
xylophone, it produces a warm, resonant tone.

*See also:* [[*idiophone][idiophone]]

** balungan
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: balungan
:SHORT: The core melodic framework of a Javanese gamelan composition.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: non-western
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balungan
:END:
In Javanese gamelan music, the balungan is the skeletal melody that
forms the structural basis of a composition. Other instruments in the
ensemble elaborate, ornament, and subdivide the balungan according to
their role in the texture. The balungan is typically played by
metallophone instruments such as the saron and slenthem.

*See also:* [[*gamelan][gamelan]], [[*slendro][slendro]], [[*pelog][pelog]]

** bandish
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: bandish
:SHORT: A fixed, composed piece within Hindustani classical music, providing the rhythmic and melodic framework for improvisation.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: non-western
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandish
:END:
In North Indian (Hindustani) classical music, a bandish (also called
gat) is a composed, fixed piece that establishes the rhythmic cycle
(tala) and provides material for improvisation. After the free-rhythm
alap, the bandish introduces the tabla and marks the transition to
metered playing.

*See also:* [[*alap][alap]], [[*raga][raga]]

** bansuri
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: bansuri
:SHORT: A transverse bamboo flute used in North Indian classical music.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: non-western
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bansuri
:END:
A side-blown bamboo flute central to Hindustani classical music and
associated with the deity Krishna. It has no keys — pitch and microtonal
inflection are controlled entirely through embouchure and finger
technique. The virtuoso Hariprasad Chaurasia is among the instrument's
most celebrated modern exponents.

** bass
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: bass
:SHORT: The lowest register of pitch; also a family of low-pitched instruments.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: myp1 myp2 myp4 dp
:CATS: homograph
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bass_(music)
:END:
In music, "bass" (rhymes with "face") refers to the low register of
sound, the lowest voice in a harmonic texture, or instruments and voices
that occupy that range. Distinct from the fish (rhymes with "mass").
The bass voice is the lowest male singing voice; bass instruments include
double bass, bass guitar, bassoon, tuba, and bass clarinet.

Note: This entry overrides the fish pronunciation globally. Use the
inline bracket syntax [[text][/ipa/]] for fish references in source files.

*See also:* [[*basso continuo][basso continuo]], [[*bass clef][bass clef]], [[*walking bass][walking bass]]

** basso continuo
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: basso-continuo
:SHORT: The harmonic bass-line foundation of Baroque music, typically played by keyboard and bass instrument together.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: italian
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basso_continuo
:END:
Italian: "continuous bass." A Baroque performance practice in which a
bass line (played by cello, bass viol, or bassoon) is combined with a
keyboard instrument (harpsichord, organ, lute) that improvises harmony
above the written bass, guided by figured bass notation. The continuo
group provides the harmonic and rhythmic foundation for nearly all
Baroque ensemble music.

*See also:* [[*figured bass][figured bass]], [[*ground bass][ground bass]], [[*walking bass][walking bass]]

** beat
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: beat
:SHORT: The basic unit of musical time that listeners tap along to.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: myp1 myp2 myp3 myp4 myp5 dp
:CATS: general
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beat_(music)
:END:
The basic recurring pulse of music — what listeners tap their feet to.
Beats are organized into metres (groups of strong and weak beats).
The tempo indicates how fast the beat moves (beats per minute, BPM).
In most popular music, beats 2 and 4 in 4/4 metre are accented (the
backbeat).

*See also:* [[*metre][metre]], [[*tempo][tempo]], [[*pulse][pulse]]

** binary form
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: binary-form
:SHORT: A two-part musical structure: A–B.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: general
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_form
:END:
A musical form consisting of two sections (A and B), usually both
repeated. Simple binary form has two contrasting sections; rounded
binary has a return of the opening material near the end of the B
section (anticipating ternary and sonata form). Characteristic of
Baroque dance movements (sarabande, allemande, etc.).

*See also:* [[*ternary form][ternary form]], [[*sonata form][sonata form]]

** blue notes
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: blue-notes
:SHORT: Flattened or bent pitches (particularly the 3rd, 5th, and 7th) characteristic of the blues.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: myp4 dp
:CATS: general
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_note
:END:
Notes that are flattened or bent slightly below the standard pitch of
a major scale, particularly the third, fifth, and seventh scale degrees.
Central to the blues and derived styles (jazz, R&B, rock). On instruments
capable of continuous pitch variation (guitar, voice, harmonica), blue
notes are bent rather than played at a fixed pitch, giving them
expressive ambiguity between major and minor.

*See also:* [[*blues scale][blues scale]], [[*bent notes][bent notes]], [[*microtone][microtone]]

** broken chord
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: broken-chord
:SHORT: The notes of a chord played one at a time rather than simultaneously.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: myp2 dp
:CATS: general
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arpeggio
:END:
A chord whose notes are played in sequence rather than simultaneously.
Used interchangeably with arpeggio, though some distinguish between the
two: an arpeggio moves consistently upward or downward, while a broken
chord may move in a freer or repeating pattern (e.g., Alberti bass).

*See also:* [[*arpeggio][arpeggio]]

** cadence
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: cadence
:SHORT: A harmonic progression that creates a sense of arrival, pause, or conclusion.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: harmony
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadence_(music)
:END:
A sequence of two or more chords that creates a sense of arrival,
punctuation, or conclusion. The four main cadence types in tonal music
are: authentic/perfect (V–I, strongest close), plagal (IV–I, "Amen"),
half/imperfect (ending on V), and deceptive/interrupted (V resolving to
vi or another unexpected chord).

*See also:* [[*authentic (perfect) cadence][authentic cadence]], [[*plagal cadence][plagal cadence]], [[*half (imperfect) cadence][half cadence]], [[*deceptive (interrupted) cadence][deceptive cadence]]

** cadenza
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: cadenza
:SHORT: An unaccompanied virtuosic passage for the soloist, typically near the end of a concerto movement.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: general
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadenza
:END:
Italian: "cadence." A virtuosic solo passage, usually unaccompanied,
occurring near the end of a movement in a concerto. The orchestra
pauses on a sustained chord (typically a 6/4 chord on the dominant)
while the soloist improvises or performs a written-out cadenza before
resolving to the final cadence. Historical cadenzas were improvised;
later composers wrote them out.

*See also:* [[*concerto][concerto]], [[*cadence][cadence]]

** canon
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: canon
:SHORT: A contrapuntal form in which the same melody is imitated by subsequent voices at a fixed time interval.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: general
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_(music)
:END:
A contrapuntal technique in which a melody in one voice is exactly
imitated by one or more following voices entering at a fixed time
interval (and sometimes at a different pitch). The strictest form of
imitation. Pachelbel's Canon in D, "Frère Jacques", and "Row Your Boat"
are familiar examples. A round is a canon in which the imitation is
at the unison or octave.

*See also:* [[*imitation][imitation]], [[*fugue][fugue]], [[*counterpoint][counterpoint]]

** cantabile
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: cantabile
:SHORT: In a singing, lyrical style.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: italian
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantabile
:END:
Italian: "singable, in a singing style." A direction to perform in a
smooth, lyrical, song-like manner, with expressive phrasing. Often
applied to instrumental music to indicate that the player should imitate
the quality and flow of a singing voice.

** cantata
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: cantata
:SHORT: A multi-movement vocal work for voices and instruments.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: italian
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantata
:END:
Italian: "sung piece." A vocal composition of several movements for
soloists, chorus, and instruments, typically setting a sacred or secular
text. Bach's church cantatas are the most celebrated examples of the
genre. Distinguished from the oratorio primarily by its shorter length
and (often) liturgical function.

*See also:* [[*aria][aria]], [[*recitative][recitative]], [[*chorale][chorale]]

** chorale
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: chorale
:SHORT: A hymn tune, especially one harmonized in four voices; also a choir.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: harmony
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chorale
:END:
(1) A hymn tune of the German Lutheran tradition, typically in four-part
(SATB) harmony. Bach's chorale harmonizations are among the most
studied examples of tonal voice leading. (2) In some usages, a choral
ensemble or the choral section of a larger work.

*See also:* [[*cantata][cantata]], [[*harmony][harmony]]

** chordophone
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: chordophone
:SHORT: An instrument that produces sound through vibrating strings.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: myp2 dp
:CATS: general
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chordophone
:END:
One of the four primary instrument categories in the Hornbostel-Sachs
classification system. Chordophones produce sound through the vibration
of one or more stretched strings. Subdivided into simple chordophones
(musical bows, zithers) and composite chordophones (lutes, harps,
lyres). Includes guitar, violin, piano, harp, and sitar.

*See also:* [[*idiophone][idiophone]], [[*membranophone][membranophone]], [[*aerophone][aerophone]]

** chromatic
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: chromatic
:SHORT: Using notes or intervals outside the prevailing diatonic scale.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: general
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_scale
:END:
Relating to pitches, intervals, or harmonies that include notes outside
a given diatonic scale. The chromatic scale divides the octave into
twelve equal semitones. Chromatic notes add color and tension;
chromaticism is characteristic of late Romantic music (Wagner, Liszt)
and jazz harmony.

*See also:* [[*diatonic][diatonic]], [[*enharmonic][enharmonic]], [[*modulation (in music)][modulation]]

** coda
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: coda
:SHORT: A concluding section added to the end of a formal structure.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: italian
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coda_(music)
:END:
Italian: "tail." A passage that brings a movement or section to a
conclusion, following the main formal structure. In sonata form, the
coda follows the recapitulation and may be brief or substantially
extended (as in Beethoven). The coda symbol (𝄌) is used in lead sheets
and popular music to indicate a jump to a concluding section.

*See also:* [[*sonata form][sonata form]], [[*recapitulation][recapitulation]]

** compound metre
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: compound-metre
:SHORT: A metre in which each beat divides into three equal parts (e.g. 6/8, 9/8, 12/8).
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: general
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre_(music)

** interval
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: interval
:SHORT: The pitch distance between two notes.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: myp1 dp
:CATS: general
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interval_(music)
:END:
The distance in pitch between two notes, measured in semitones and
named by their scale-degree distance. Basic intervals include unison,
second, third, fourth, fifth, sixth, seventh, and octave, each further
qualified as major, minor, perfect, augmented, or diminished.
Intervals are the building blocks of melody, harmony, and counterpoint.

** inversion
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: inversion
:SHORT: (1) A chord with a note other than the root in the bass; (2) a melody flipped upside down.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: harmony
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inversion_(music)
:END:
The term has two related meanings. (1) Chord inversion: a chord arranged
so that a note other than the root is in the lowest voice. First
inversion has the third in the bass; second inversion has the fifth.
(2) Melodic inversion: a melody restated with all intervals flipped
(ascending intervals become descending and vice versa). Both uses are
important in counterpoint and tonal harmony.

:SLUG: voicing
:SHORT: The specific arrangement of notes within a chord, including register and spacing.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: myp2 dp
:CATS: general
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voicing_(music)
:END:
The distribution of the notes of a chord among the available voices or
instruments, including decisions about register, spacing, and doubling.
Two chords may contain the same pitches but differ significantly in
character depending on how those pitches are arranged. Closely spaced
voicings (close position) produce a dense sound; widely spaced voicings
(open position) produce a more transparent texture. Voicing is a central
skill in arranging, piano comping, and choral writing.

** walking bass
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: walking-bass
:SHORT: A bass line of steady quarter notes that outlines the chord changes.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: myp4 dp
:CATS: general
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bassline
:SOURCE_2: https://jazznightschool.org/pages/walking-bass-lines-introduction-part-1

A style of bass accompaniment common in jazz, blues, and Baroque music,

** wavetable synthesis
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: wavetable-synthesis
:SHORT: A synthesis method that cycles through stored digital waveforms to produce sound.
:IMPORTANCE: expert
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: general
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavetable_synthesis
:SOURCE_2: https://blog.native-instruments.com/what-is-wavetable-synthesis/
:END:
A digital synthesis technique in which a stored table of single-cycle
waveforms is read repeatedly to produce a tone. What distinguishes
wavetable synthesis from simple sampling is the ability to morph or
scan through multiple waveforms over time, creating evolving timbres.
Invented by Max Mathews in 1958 and developed for hardware synthesizers
by Wolfgang Palm (PPG) in the late 1970s, the technique became
ubiquitous in software synthesis through instruments such as Native
Instruments Massive and Xfer Serum.

*See also:* [[*subtractive synthesis][subtractive synthesis]], [[*FM synthesis][FM synthesis]], [[*granular synthesis][granular synthesis]]

** wet/dry signal
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: wet-dry-signal
:SHORT: Dry = unprocessed audio; wet = audio with effects applied.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: myp3 myp4 myp5 dp
:CATS: general
:SOURCE: https://www.differencebetween.net/technology/difference-between-wet-and-dry-signals-or-sounds/
:SOURCE_2: https://asmr.education/faq/music-mixing/dry-signal-vs-wet-signal
:END:
A dry signal is the original, unprocessed audio as captured or generated
before any effects are applied. A wet signal is that same audio after
passing through one or more effects processors — reverb, delay, chorus,
distortion, etc. In most effects units and plugins, a wet/dry (or
mix) knob blends the two, allowing the producer to dial in the amount
of effect without replacing the source sound entirely.

** 12-bar blues
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: 12-bar-blues
:SHORT: A repeating 12-measure harmonic framework foundational to blues, jazz, and rock.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: myp4 dp
:CATS: general
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve-bar_blues
:END:
A repeating harmonic and formal pattern of twelve bars that is the
foundational framework of blues music and has influenced jazz, rhythm
and blues, rock and roll, and country. The basic form uses three chords:
I (bars 1–4), IV (bars 5–6), I (bars 7–8), V (bar 9), IV (bar 10),
I (bars 11–12). Countless variations exist, particularly in jazz
(the "rhythm changes" is a contrasting alternative).

** 12-note techniques
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: 12-note-techniques
:SHORT: A compositional method using a fixed ordering of all twelve chromatic pitches as the basis for a piece.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: general
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve-tone_technique
:END:
A compositional method developed by Arnold Schoenberg in the early
1920s in which all twelve chromatic pitches are arranged into a fixed
ordered series (tone row or set). The row and its three transformations
— inversion (I), retrograde (R), and retrograde inversion (RI) — form
the exclusive pitch material of the piece. Adopted and developed by
Webern and Berg (the Second Viennese School) and later by Stravinsky,
Dallapiccola, and others.

*See also:* [[*atonal][atonal]], [[*retrograde][retrograde]], [[*inversion][inversion]]

* Roman numerals

** VII
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: vii
:SHORT: The seventh scale degree or a chord built on it.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: roman-numeral
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_numeral_analysis
:END:
In Roman numeral analysis, VII (or vii°) denotes the seventh degree of
the scale and the chord built on it. In major keys, the seventh degree
is a leading tone a half step below the tonic; the triad built on it is
diminished. Uppercase numerals indicate major quality; lowercase indicate
minor.

** VI
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: vi
:SHORT: The sixth scale degree or a chord built on it.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: myp4 myp5 dp
:CATS: roman-numeral
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_numeral_analysis
:END:
VI denotes the sixth scale degree (the submediant) and the chord built
on it. In major keys, this chord is minor (vi); when written in uppercase
(VI), it refers to a major chord on the sixth degree, common in minor
keys and as a chromatic alteration.

** IV
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: iv
:SHORT: The subdominant — the fourth scale degree or a chord built on it.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: myp2 myp4 myp5 dp
:CATS: roman-numeral
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_numeral_analysis
:END:
IV denotes the subdominant — the fourth degree of the scale. The
subdominant chord moves naturally toward either the dominant (V) or
directly to the tonic (I), the latter producing a plagal cadence.

** V
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: v
:SHORT: The dominant — the fifth scale degree or a chord built on it.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: myp2 myp4 myp5 dp
:CATS: roman-numeral
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_numeral_analysis
:END:
V denotes the dominant — the fifth degree of the scale and the most
harmonically active chord in tonal music. The dominant triad (V) or
seventh chord (V7) creates strong tension that resolves to the tonic (I).
The V–I progression is the foundation of tonal cadential motion.

** III
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: iii
:SHORT: The mediant — the third scale degree or a chord built on it.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: roman-numeral
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_numeral_analysis
:END:
III denotes the mediant — the third degree of the scale, midway between
the tonic and dominant. In major keys the mediant chord is minor (iii);
uppercase III refers to a major chord on the third degree, typical in
minor keys.

** II
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: ii
:SHORT: The supertonic — the second scale degree or a chord built on it.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: dp
:CATS: roman-numeral
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_numeral_analysis
:END:
II denotes the supertonic — the second degree of the scale. In major
keys, the supertonic chord is minor (ii) and functions as a
pre-dominant, often preceding V. The II chord (uppercase, major) appears
frequently as a secondary dominant or in modal contexts.

** I
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: i
:SHORT: The tonic — the home chord, built on the first scale degree.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: myp2 myp4 myp5 dp
:CATS: roman-numeral
:SOURCE: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_numeral_analysis
:END:
I denotes the tonic — the first scale degree and the chord built on it.
The tonic chord is the harmonic center of gravity in tonal music; most
pieces begin and end on I. In major keys it is a major triad; in minor
keys it is minor (i).

* Music Learning Theory

** Audiation
:PROPERTIES:
:SHORT: Hearing and understanding music in your mind when the sound is not there.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: all
:CATS: mlt
:SEE: tonal-pattern rhythm-pattern resting-tone
:SOURCE: gordon-lsm
:LINK: /practice/audiation.html
:END:
Gordon: "Audiation is to music what thought is to language" (Introduction). It is "the process of assimilating and comprehending (not simply rehearing) music momentarily heard performed or heard sometime in the past", and also happens when reading, composing or improvising (p. 3). Imitation is not audiation: "Imitation is learning through someone else's ears. Audiation is learning through one's own ears" (p. 9).

** Tonal pattern
:PROPERTIES:
:SHORT: Two to five pitches heard as one unit, like a word in language.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: all
:CATS: mlt
:SEE: audiation rhythm-pattern
:SOURCE: gordon-lsm
:LINK: /practice/patterns.html
:END:
"Just as a word is the basic unit of meaning in language, so a pattern is the basic unit of meaning in music" (p. 99). Students first learn patterns of "at least two but not more than five pitches corresponding to important functions in a tonality", such as tonic and dominant (p. 100). Tonal patterns are learned apart from rhythm, every pitch the same length.

** Rhythm pattern
:PROPERTIES:
:SHORT: A group of durations heard as one unit, organized around macrobeats and microbeats.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: all
:CATS: mlt
:SEE: tonal-pattern macrobeat
:SOURCE: gordon-lsm
:LINK: /practice/rhythms.html
:END:
Learned apart from pitch, chanted on one pitch with expressive inflection (p. 101).

** Resting tone
:PROPERTIES:
:SHORT: The pitch a melody feels at rest on; it tells you the tonality.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: all
:CATS: mlt
:SEE: tonal-tonality audiation
:SOURCE: gordon-lsm
:LINK: /theory/modes-three-ways.html
:END:
In MLT, tonality is heard from its resting tone, not read from a key signature (p. 150).

** Macrobeat
:PROPERTIES:
:SHORT: The longer, stronger beats you feel the music moving in; their grouping gives the meter.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: all
:CATS: mlt rhythm
:SEE: microbeat rhythm-pattern metre
:SOURCE: gordon-lsm
:LINK: /theory/meter-taxonomy.html
:END:
Macrobeats divided into two microbeats give usual duple meter; into three, usual triple (pp. 175–176).

** Microbeat
:PROPERTIES:
:SHORT: The equal divisions of a macrobeat: two in duple meter, three in triple.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: all
:CATS: mlt rhythm
:SEE: macrobeat
:SOURCE: gordon-lsm
:END:

** Skill learning sequence
:PROPERTIES:
:SHORT: Gordon's order for learning music skills: from hearing and singing, to naming, to reading, to inventing and explaining.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: all
:CATS: mlt
:SEE: discrimination-learning inference-learning aural-oral
:SOURCE: gordon-lsm
:LINK: /course/enrichment.html
:END:
Two kinds of learning. /Discrimination learning/ (you are taught): aural/oral, verbal association, partial synthesis, symbolic association, composite synthesis. /Inference learning/ (you teach yourself from what you know): generalization, creativity/improvisation, theoretical understanding. Students move up step by step, and may bridge across to inference levels once ready (Table 17, p. 230). On this site the enrichment options are tagged with these levels.

** Discrimination learning
:PROPERTIES:
:SHORT: The levels of skill learning where you are taught: learning by rote, then naming, reading and writing what you know.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: all
:CATS: mlt
:SEE: skill-learning-sequence inference-learning
:SOURCE: gordon-lsm
:END:

** Inference learning
:PROPERTIES:
:SHORT: The levels of skill learning where you teach yourself unfamiliar music from what you already know.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: all
:CATS: mlt
:SEE: skill-learning-sequence discrimination-learning
:SOURCE: gordon-lsm
:END:
"It is impossible to teach students all patterns they will ultimately need to know … by relying on discrimination learning alone" (p. 106).

** Aural/oral
:PROPERTIES:
:SLUG: aural-oral
:SHORT: MLT level 1: hear patterns and sing or chant them back on a neutral syllable, with no names or notation.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: all
:CATS: mlt mlt-level
:SEE: verbal-association
:SOURCE: gordon-lsm
:END:
A pause comes between the teacher's pattern and the student's answer, "because without a pause, students are encouraged to imitate, not audiate" (p. 102). Scales, names, notation and theory come later (pp. 101–102).

** Verbal association
:PROPERTIES:
:SHORT: MLT level 2: put names on patterns you can already audiate: tonal and rhythm syllables, then names like major or duple.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: all
:CATS: mlt mlt-level
:SEE: aural-oral partial-synthesis solf-ge
:SOURCE: gordon-lsm
:END:
Movable do with a la-based minor, and rhythm syllables based on beat functions (p. 105). Sounds are taught first, then syllables as names for what has already been heard (p. 107).

** Partial synthesis
:PROPERTIES:
:SHORT: MLT level 3: recognize the tonality or meter of a series of familiar patterns, with no context given first.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: all
:CATS: mlt mlt-level
:SEE: verbal-association symbolic-association
:SOURCE: gordon-lsm
:END:
The teacher uses a neutral syllable so that students find the tonality or meter through audiation, not from the syllables (pp. 111–117).

** Symbolic association
:PROPERTIES:
:SHORT: MLT level 4: read and write the notation of patterns you can already audiate and name.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: all
:CATS: mlt mlt-level
:SEE: partial-synthesis composite-synthesis notation
:SOURCE: gordon-lsm
:END:
Notation is "a 'window' one sees through. Audiation is on the other side" (p. 8). From about p. 117.

** Composite synthesis
:PROPERTIES:
:SHORT: MLT level 5: read and write series of patterns and recognize their tonality or meter in notation.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: all
:CATS: mlt mlt-level
:SEE: symbolic-association generalization
:SOURCE: gordon-lsm
:END:
From p. 126.

** Generalization
:PROPERTIES:
:SHORT: MLT inference level: identify unfamiliar patterns by ear, by name or in notation, using what you know.
:IMPORTANCE: core
:LEVELS: all
:CATS: mlt mlt-level
:SEE: composite-synthesis creativity-improvisation
:SOURCE: gordon-lsm
:END:
Sublevels: aural/oral, verbal and symbolic (reading and writing) (pp. 129–133).

** Creativity/improvisation
:PROPERTIES:

** Theoretical understanding
:PROPERTIES:
:SHORT: MLT inference level: explain why music works as it does, the last level, built on audiation.
:IMPORTANCE: enriching
:LEVELS: all
:CATS: mlt mlt-level

Music theory comes at the end of the sequence, not the beginning (p. 106). From about p. 141.
