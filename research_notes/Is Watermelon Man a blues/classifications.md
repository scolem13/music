# How published sources classify Herbie Hancock's "Watermelon Man" with respect to the blues

Note on method: quotes below come from WebFetch page summaries (a small model extracting text), so treat wording as near-exact but not guaranteed verbatim. No print source (Gioia, Real Book, Sher, Aebersold, Grove, Penguin, Pond, Gluck) could be read directly.

## 1. Lead-sheet collections, databases, play-alongs, Gioia

### Takeaway
No lead-sheet or book source could be verified. Every web database/education page that states a form calls it a 16-bar blues (or "altered"/"extended" blues). Gioia's entry (p. 446) exists but its wording was not obtained.

### Cited Findings
- Standard Repertoire (standardrepertoire.com) lists length "16 bars" and subtitles it "Altered blues recorded by Herbie as a hard bop song, and later in a fusion style"; it lists style as "Swing" (an oddity). — [Standard Repertoire](https://standardrepertoire.com/watermelon-man.html)
- AllSolos lists form as "Blues - 16 bars", "built on an extended blues structure", "bluesy, danceable riff". — [AllSolos](https://www.allsolos.com/tune/77/watermelon-man)
- Learn Jazz Standards: "This 16 bar blues is played in a straight eight groove, originally a latin-rock style, and later funk." — [learnjazzstandards.com](https://learnjazzstandards.com/?p=6627)
- Ted Gioia, The Jazz Standards (Oxford, 2012): a Google Books catalog page indicates the Watermelon Man entry is on p. 446 (per search-tool summary). Entry text NOT obtained. — [Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Jazz_Standards.html?id=oPuMQx9GZVcC)
- Swiss-jazz.ch lead sheet PDF (Flavio Goulart de Andrade, 2007) exists; its search snippet shows changes F7 / B-flat 7 / C7 pattern, but the PDF is image-only and could not be read or verified. — [PDF](https://www.swiss-jazz.ch/standards-jazz/Watermelon.pdf)

### Inferences
- Amateur/educational databases converge on "16-bar blues" with qualifiers "altered" or "extended"; none say "not a blues".

### Gaps
- Real Book (Hal Leonard), New Real Book (Sher), iReal Pro, jazzstandards.com (search returned no page), Aebersold volumes, Hal Leonard Play-Along: no labels found/verifiable.
- Gioia's exact wording unavailable.

## 2. Music-theory and jazz-education texts and sites

### Takeaway
Education sites teach it as a 16-bar blues: bars 1-8 like a normal blues, bars 9-16 an extended/varied ending. Wikipedia's "Sixteen-bar blues" cites it only uncited.

### Cited Findings
- Jazzfuel (Nick Lea): "essentially a sixteen-bar blues"; "the first 8 bars mimic a classic blues exactly"; "the following 8 are an elongated variation of the regular final section"; "a healthy dose of the blues with a bit of gospel thrown in"; also "'straight-eighths' latin-rock style groove" and "an early example of what would become known as Latin boogaloo". — [Jazzfuel](https://jazzfuel.com/watermelon-man/)
- FretDojo: "it's a 16 bar blues, rather than a 12 bar blues" (extra changes from bar 8 to 12); piano riff "combines elements of bebop, jazz and R&B – with a pop twist to boot." — [FretDojo](https://fretdojo.com/watermelon-man-guitar/)
- Wikipedia "Sixteen-bar blues": "This can be heard, for example, in 'Watermelon Man' by Herbie Hancock." No inline citation; the preceding variation sentence is tagged [citation needed]; none of the three listed references (Horn 1994 review; Hal Leonard "The Blues" 1995 pp. 112-113) mention the tune. Trace ends there: unsourced. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixteen-bar_blues)
- Old Wikipedia mirror (2006 snapshot): 16-bar form "is considered an elongated 12 bar blues" (via search summary; I did not open the page). — [mirror](https://wikipedia2006.classicistranieri.com/en/w/a/t/Watermelon_Man_%28song%29_7249.html)
- Harmonica forum post (via search summary, unopened): first eight bars match a 12-bar blues; the second eight alternate IV and V three times. — [forum](https://www.modernbluesharmonica.com/board/board_topic/5560960/4466294.htm)
- Jazzguitar.be lesson/forum exist but returned 403/blog not read directly. — [jazzguitar.be](https://jazzguitar.be/blog/watermelon-man-guitar/)

### Inferences
- The "elongated 12-bar blues" framing (bars 1-8 blues-like) is the common teaching line; it is a form claim, not a genre claim.

### Gaps
- Mark Levine, Berklee, Open Music Theory, musictheory.net, StudyBass, school band arrangements: nothing found.

## 3. Encyclopedias and critics; anyone arguing it is not a blues

### Takeaway
Critics use genre labels (hard bop, soul jazz, funk, jazz-funk, boogaloo) and "bluesy"; none found calls it a 12/16-bar "blues" formally, and none found denies it. No dissent located.

### Cited Findings
- Wikipedia (composition): "'Watermelon Man' is written in a sixteen-bar blues form." (uncited); infobox genres "Hard bop", "soul jazz" (uncited); "The tune drew on elements of R&B, soul jazz, and bebop" (Creswell, 1001 Songs, 2006, p. 333); first version "recorded in a hard bop style" (Brackett, New Rolling Stone Album Guide, 2004, p. 361); Head Hunters "funk version" (Brackett); Santamaria cha-cha (Gerard 2001); boogaloo origin (Flores 2000, p. 93); "over two hundred" recordings (Santoro 2004 p. 86). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watermelon_Man_(composition))
- Wikipedia (Takin' Off): "The bluesy track 'Watermelon Man'..." cited to Martin & Waters 2005, p. 311; album genres soul jazz, hard bop; AllMusic Steve Huey (2011) 4.5 stars; Penguin Guide (Cook & Morton, 9th ed. 2008, p. 640) 3.5 stars; review texts not shown. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takin%27_Off)
- Search summary of Huey's AllMusic review (reprint, not read directly): hard bop with "funky piano riffing"; Huey calls "Empty Pockets" bluesy rather than Watermelon Man. Unverified. — [Takin' Off summary](https://www.allsolos.com/album/73/takin-off)
- uDiscover (Charles Waring): Hancock quoted calling it "funky"; 1973 version "a radical jazz-funk makeover". — [uDiscover](https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/watermelon-man-herbie-hancock-feature)
- Superbrass (search snippet): "based on a 16 bar blues riff" drawing on R&B, soul, jazz, bebop, gospel. — [Superbrass](https://superbrass.co.uk/product/watermelon-man)

### Inferences
- Dominant critical frame: soul-jazz/hard bop/funk with a blues-derived form; "bluesy" is common, "a blues" is mostly in education/form descriptions.

### Gaps
- Grove, Gioia History of Jazz, Gluck, Pond (Head Hunters), actual AllMusic and Penguin text: not accessed. No source arguing "not a blues" found (absence after limited search).

## 4. Grammy Hall of Fame / Library of Congress / Blue Note

### Takeaway
Only the Grammy Hall of Fame fact (Santamaria's version, inducted 1998) turned up; no official description of its form.

### Cited Findings
- Santamaria's version inducted into Grammy Hall of Fame 1998, #10 Hot 100 April 1963 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watermelon_Man_(composition))

### Gaps
- Library of Congress (National Recording Registry) and Blue Note descriptions: not found.

## Overall for the "(Blues)" label decision
- Support: nearly all form descriptions say 16-bar blues; sole concern is that it is an extended/altered one (bars 9-16 vary the 12-bar ending) and genre labels are soul jazz/funk/boogaloo.
- Weakest link: Wikipedia sixteen-bar page unsourced; print authorities unverified.
